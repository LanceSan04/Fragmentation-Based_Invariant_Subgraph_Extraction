## Main conclusions

1. A reproducible endpoint-order sensitivity remains in the fragment scorer. This is the highest-priority correctness experiment.
2. Shared and independent validation selected identical epochs for every learned-model seed in this study. Changing between those checkpoint-selection policies cannot explain this study's learned-versus-ERM test gap.
3. Actual atom/bond retention differs considerably from the configured connection ratio. Views overlap substantially; pictures alone overstate how distinct they may be.
4. Our contrastive objective differs from the official SuGAr implementation. This is an architectural/objective difference, not evidence by itself of a bug or of the cause of poor AUC.
5. Validation combines many assay groups, almost half of which contain only one class. Pooled AUC should be accompanied by carefully qualified within-assay analysis.
6. An isolated tied-score fixture reproduces batch-dependent top-k selection. This is a second concrete correctness/reproducibility issue; its contribution to real prediction changes is not yet isolated.

## Ranked findings

### 1. Fragment scorer depends on endpoint ordering

**Severity: High. Status: reproduced. Performance impact: unmeasured.**

Evidence: `src/models/fragment_masker.py:134-148`, `FragmentMasker.forward`. Encoder edges are symmetrized, but the scorer concatenates `[h[src], h[dst], directed edge attributes]`. Reversing fragment IDs and consistently swapping endpoint features preserves chemistry while changing the scorer's ordered input. Git blame attributes the ordered concatenation to `1fdd9a8a`; encoder symmetrization is from `4389ca65`. This is a longstanding property, not proven to be the later regression's origin.

Controlled validation-only probe: seed 8101, 100 molecules selected evenly across ranks of fragment-connection count, five views each. Reversing IDs changed selected bonds in **295/500** view–molecule cases and at least one view in **91/100** molecules. Largest score change: **0.3986**. See `view_behavior_relabel/diagnostics.json`. The experiment uses the same saved weights; no training or test selection.

Mechanism: arbitrary fragment numbering can affect which chemistry the classifier sees. Encoding both directions does not make an ordered edge-scoring MLP symmetric.

Proposed fix: average the scorer's logits for both endpoint orientations, swapping the corresponding feature blocks, or adopt a symmetric edge representation. Add fragment-ID permutation tests for scores, selected original bonds, and final predictions. Do not silently modify inference on old benchmark checkpoints: train a separately labelled matched arm. Validate tie behavior separately.

### 2. Contrastive objective is pooled across views rather than summed per view

**Severity: Medium. Status: confirmed implementation difference. Performance impact: hypothesis.**

Evidence: `src/training/sugar_trainer.py:181-198`, `_compute_components` (blame `a0e86001`), concatenates all view embeddings and repeats labels, then computes one mean contrastive loss. Prediction loss is summed over views. Official `main.py:423-429,478-498` computes contrastive loss separately for each model and sums model losses.

Consequences: our positive/negative pair set includes cross-view pairs, including the same molecule across views. The relative reduction also changes with view count. Thus the same lambda is not equivalent to SuGAr's lambda, and a comment describing all per-view terms as summed does not describe this contrastive path accurately. It may encourage representation agreement while structural diversity is desired; this is a hypothesis, not proof of harm.

Small validation experiment: fixed data, seeds, budget and policy; compare current pooled contrastive objective with a sum of per-view objectives, logging weighted gradient norms. Include prediction-only control. Do not change several coefficients or model components simultaneously.

### 3. Connection ratios are poor proxies for retained chemical information

**Severity: Medium. Status: measured design property, not necessarily a bug.**

Evidence: `src/models/fragment_masker.py:34-76`, `segment_topk_mask`; `src/models/fragment_edge_sampler.py:47-64`; `src/models/atom_reconstructor.py:104-180`.

Training samples candidates, then sizes top-k from the original connection count, capped by eligible candidates. Evaluation exposes all candidates. At sample ratio 0.7 and edge ratio 0.5 the requested selected count matches between train and evaluation for all 4,540 training molecules. However, **196/4,540** molecules select every sampled candidate, leaving no ranking choice within that sampled set (soft gating can still learn).

A 100-molecule size-stratified rank sample with seed 8101 retained **62.51% of atoms** and **58.15% of bonds** on average despite selecting roughly half the fragment connections. Atom retention ranged **11.11–100%**. Selected atom graphs averaged **2.60 connected components**. These are descriptive results from one checkpoint/sample, not independent replications or dataset-wide estimates.

Proposed next experiment: ratio response curves in terms of actual atom/bond retention and connectivity. Changing ratios on a frozen model is diagnostic only; evaluating a new ratio's predictive benefit requires matched training. Do not assume a ratio copied from atom-edge SuGAr has the same information budget at fragment level.

### 4. View differences are real but often overlap

**Severity: Medium. Status: measured; not automatically a defect.**

On the same 100 molecules, across ten view pairs per molecule: mean atom Jaccard **0.7576**, bond Jaccard **0.7352**. Jaccard is intersection size divided by union size: 1 means identical, 0 means disjoint. **313/1,000** pairs retained identical atom sets and **300/1,000** identical bond sets. Pairs sharing a molecule/checkpoint are dependent observations; these counts are descriptive, not 1,000 independent statistical samples.

Different structures do not necessarily imply useful prediction diversity; identical atom sets can have different bond choices/weights. Add prediction correlation and error complementarity across multiple saved seeds before deciding to enable diversity loss.

Existing `src/experiments/views.py:40-61` assumes aligned fragment-edge spaces when comparing scores/masks. Mixed fragmentations may error or compare unrelated columns. This does not affect the latest all-Group-Graph study. The new diagnostic compares original atom/bond IDs, avoiding this alignment assumption for overlap. A future fix should restrict edge-score correlation to compatible methods and explicitly label the shared-policy reference.

### 5. Assay composition and checkpoint peaks need separate analysis

**Severity: Medium. Status: measured. Causal explanation of test gap: unresolved.**

Train: **4,540 molecules, 47 assays, 74.45% positive**, 20 single-class assays. Validation: **2,572 molecules, 46 assays, 89.11% positive**, 22 single-class assays. No shared assay IDs or identical RDKit-canonical SMILES were found across these two caches. This exact-identity check does not establish absence of near-duplicate chemistry, stereochemical equivalence, or all forms of leakage.

Pooled ROC-AUC includes cross-assay positive/negative comparisons. It can therefore differ from performance within an assay. Single-class assays have undefined within-assay AUC. Report usable-assay counts and sizes; prevalence differences alone do not mathematically imply an AUC change.

Learned shared/independent selected epochs: **62,18,22,50,21**, identical under both policies. ERM selected epochs: **9,7,9,9,10**. Learned independent validation peaks exceed their surrounding +/-2-epoch average by about **0.0107–0.0224 AUC**. ERM also has local peaks. This is not proof of noisy measurement or that smoothing helps test performance. Different epochs have different weights. See `curve_summary.json` and `validation_curves.png`.

Next: investigate within-assay performance and representation/retention shifts using development data; evaluate a predeclared selection rule on additional held-out training domains if needed. Avoid choosing a smoothing window from test scores.

### 6. Logging is substantial but not a universal resolved-runtime specification

**Severity: Low/Medium. Status: confirmed gaps in traceability.**

Evidence: `src/training/checkpoint.py:12-27,35-74`; `src/utils/run_outputs.py`, `write_effective_config`; `src/experiments/selection.py:49-65`; `src/experiments/visualize_views.py`.

Recent runs save supplied config, checkpoints, feature dimensions, selected epochs, commands and verified source records. However, defaults inside constructors are not necessarily materialized in config. The dual-policy runner's base config says `inference_policy=shared` while running both policies; checkpoint configs and results provide the actual distinction. Base-config flags such as `compare_selection_policies=false` should not be interpreted as the runner's effective behavior. Environment/library versions and runtime switches are not universally captured by every path.

The visualization manifest now includes checkpoint config, effective execution config and sampler seeds. The new diagnostics record their sampling rule and checkpoint digest. Recommended future improvement: one resolved run specification shared by all entry points, with explicit checkpoint-selection policy, prediction policy, defaults, package versions and data/source hashes. Do not infer behavior solely from the YAML.

## Additional confirmed finding: top-k ties depend on batch composition

**Severity: High for reproducibility; AUC impact unmeasured.** `src/models/fragment_masker.py:79` sorts all scores without `stable=True`, before a stable sort by graph. Equal scores may arrive in a different within-graph order when unrelated graphs are added. An isolated fixture with scores `[1,1,1,0,1,0,0]` selects positions `[0,1,2]` alone but `[0,2,4]` when appended to an unrelated graph. See `topk_tie_probe_mixed.json` for the complete deterministic input. The simplest all-equal fixture did not fail; the mixed fixture does.

Separately, seed-8101 learned CPU evaluation yielded AUC 0.806126 at batch size 1 and 0.806226 at batch size 64, versus saved GPU AUC 0.806100. Across CPU batch sizes, 92 molecules changed probability by more than 1e-4; five changed by more than 0.01; maximum change was 0.04466. Batch64 CPU differs from saved GPU by 0.0001254, slightly exceeding the earlier 1e-4 parity tolerance. Do not replace the frozen GPU benchmark result with these diagnostic values. Both floating-point score perturbations and exact top-k ties can affect discrete selections; the fixture proves a tie-handling issue but does not yet attribute each real discrepancy to it.

Proposed fix: stable descending score sort with explicit original-edge-ID tie breaking. Verify masks alone versus in mixed batches and verify repeatability. This addresses batch composition, not full fragment-renumbering invariance; exact chemically equivalent ties need a separately documented policy. Keep old results frozen and mark the implementation change before further experiments.

| Arm | CPU pooled AUC | Mean usable-assay AUC | Within-assay pair-weighted AUC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Learned | 0.806226 | 0.515335 | 0.499489 |
| Random | 0.792874 | 0.516448 | 0.509629 |
| ERM | 0.766051 | 0.524046 | 0.510213 |

All arms have 24 usable assays; 22 single-class assays are excluded from within-assay AUC. Only **1.068%** of all positive-negative pairs are within the same assay. Pair-weighted AUC weights each assay by its positive-count times negative-count, while macro AUC gives each usable assay equal weight. These are different estimands from the benchmark pooled AUC, not replacement scores. Some assays have only one minority-class example; do not overinterpret individual assay ranks. This signal appears in all three arms and is not unique evidence against fragments. It suggests assay-level ranking deserves investigation; it does not prove deliberate assay-ID leakage or identify a causal mechanism for the test gap.

Full validation, all five views of seed 8101: mean atom retention **62.93%**, bond retention **58.87%**, selected component count **2.51**. Mean atom Jaccard **0.7439**, bond Jaccard **0.7226** across 25,720 dependent view pairs. Other seeds are still needed to establish stability.

## SuGAr implementation comparison

The paper's ENS design aggregates scores into a shared selected graph before combining classifier predictions. Independent voting is our alternative policy, not an exact reproduction of that ENS design. Official code uses atom-edge selection; our fragmentation/reconstruction changes selection units. Official `models/ciga.py:253-270` uses a ceiling budget, while our canonical fragment-connection selection uses floor with a minimum of one. Shared initialization exists in our builder. The diversity objectives also differ: official training uses scaled dot products after score normalization; ours uses per-molecule centered cosine over mutually eligible edges. Diversity and IRM are zero in this study, so differences in their formulas cannot directly explain this study's loss gradients.

This audit does not establish benchmark comparability of source dataset versions, hyperparameter budgets or all preprocessing choices. Individual seeds should not be compared against the paper's reported multi-run average as a superiority claim.
